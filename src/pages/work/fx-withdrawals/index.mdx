<Section title="Context">

I worked on Stake’s multi-currency withdrawal experience, helping investors move funds into international bank accounts.

International withdrawals introduced complexity that the domestic flow never had to handle: two currencies, changing exchange rates, fees, expiry states and market availability. The challenge was exposing enough financial detail to build trust without turning a simple withdrawal into a trading interface.

</Section>

<Section title="Approach">

### From PM conversation to prototype in hours

Before a PRD existed, I used an early conversation with the PM to start shaping the experience. Granola captured the discussion, then my Product & Design Copilot GPT turned those notes into structured design requirements I could prototype from straight away.

Using that requirements document as context, I explored the withdrawal flow across Magic Patterns, Figma Make and Claude. Within hours I had working concepts to review with the PM, before the formal PRD had been written. This:

- made the proposed flow tangible much earlier
- surfaced questions before requirements were locked
- helped Product and Design align on interaction logic
- accelerated the move from discussion to product definition

### Designing the withdrawal flow for trust

Once we aligned on the direction, I refined the experience around the moments where users needed the most certainty: the exchange rate, the fees, the amount they would receive and the transfer timing.

</Section>

<Section title="Outcome">

The international withdrawal experience launched with EUR, GBP and SAR, with the flow designed to support additional currencies over time. It was one of my final projects at Stake.

- **Earlier alignment:** prototypes gave Product and Design something tangible to align on before requirements were final.
- **Clearer decisions:** rates, fees and market edge cases surfaced earlier.
- **Transparent experience:** users could see what they were sending, what they would receive and when it would arrive.

</Section>
